Taking notes by hand slows you down, and that is exactly why it works. Because you cannot write down every word a lecturer says, you are forced to decide which ideas matter and to put them into your own words. ① This quiet act of selecting and rephrasing turns passive listening into active thinking. ② Studies comparing students who wrote by hand with those who typed found that the handwriters often did better on conceptual questions, even though the typists produced longer notes. ③ Typing tends to encourage word-for-word transcription, which lets information pass from the ears to the fingers without ever being processed. ④ Modern laptops have longer battery lives and lighter bodies than they did a decade ago, making them easier to carry between classes. ⑤ In other words, the limits of handwriting are not a flaw but a built-in filter that rewards understanding over copying.
